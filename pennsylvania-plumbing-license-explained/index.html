How Plumbing Licensing Works In Pennsylvania, And How To Actually Vet A Plumber

This comes up more than you'd expect, and the honest answer surprises some homeowners: Pennsylvania does not have a single statewide plumbing license. If you've searched for a license number to verify a plumber and come up confused, this is why, and here's what to actually check instead.

Municipal, Not State, Licensing

Instead of one state board, plumbing licensing in Pennsylvania is handled at the municipal or county level, and requirements vary depending on the jurisdiction. What's required in one municipality may differ from the next, and there's no single statewide database or board issuing a uniform license number the way some other states operate.

Why This Confuses People

Homeowners moving from a state with centralized licensing, or who've searched online, sometimes come across a low-quality source claiming a "Pennsylvania State Plumbing Board" exists. It doesn't, and this claim contradicts every credible source on the subject. We're not going to cite a fabricated credential just because it sounds official.

The Actual Vetting Checklist

Since there's no single statewide license number to check, here's what actually protects you when hiring a plumber in Pennsylvania:

  1. Ask if the company carries general liability insurance and, if you're comfortable asking, request to see a certificate of insurance. A legitimate company will have this ready to share without hesitation
  2. Check whether the municipality you live in requires local plumber registration, since some do even though the state doesn't. York City and several York County municipalities maintain their own registration or permit requirements, which your municipal office can confirm
  3. Ask how long the company has been operating in your specific area, not just in business generally. Local experience with your area's housing stock (like York's older boiler and clay-line homes) is a genuinely useful signal that a generic license number wouldn't tell you anyway
  4. Get a written, itemized estimate before work starts, not a verbal number. A company confident in its pricing will put it in writing without pushback
  5. Ask directly whether a permit is required for the specific work being done, and confirm whether the company pulls it themselves or expects you to. For anything beyond a basic repair, like a repipe or new water heater installation, a permit is often required and skipping it can cause problems later, especially at resale
  6. Check recent reviews specifically for how the company handled a problem, not just star ratings, since how a company responds when something goes wrong tells you more than a perfect score

Red Flags Worth Taking Seriously

  • Pressure to pay the full amount upfront before any work has started
  • Reluctance to provide a written estimate or explain what a quoted price actually includes
  • No fixed business address or only a cell phone number with no company name attached
  • Refusing to answer directly when asked about insurance

Our Approach

We're not going to invent a license number or claim a credential that doesn't exist just because it's what homeowners are used to seeing elsewhere. We'd rather be straightforward about how Pennsylvania's system actually works, carry real insurance, pull permits when the work requires one, and let our pricing transparency and communication speak for themselves.
