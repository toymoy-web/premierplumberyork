A water service line leak is specifically a leak in the underground pipe running from your water meter to your house, distinct from a leak inside the home. It's the kind of leak that often shows up first as a mysteriously high water bill or a meter that keeps running with everything in the house shut off, sometimes with no visible sign in the yard at all until the leak has been running for a while.

Local Context

Why This Line Fails In Older York Properties

Service lines installed decades ago, particularly in pre-1960 construction, were often galvanized steel or early-generation copper, both of which corrode and develop pinhole leaks over time underground. York's freeze-thaw winters add ground movement stress on top of that normal corrosion, which can accelerate a failure at a weak point in an aging line. This is common across Red Lion, PA and the rest of York County too.

Signs To Watch For

Symptoms Of A Water Service Line Leak Repair Problem

Meter Running With Everything Off

This is the clearest sign: if your meter's low-flow indicator moves with every fixture in the house off, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and your home.

Unexplained Wet Or Soft Spot In The Yard

A patch of ground that's greener, softer, or wetter than the surrounding area, especially along the likely path between the meter and the house, can point directly to the leak's location.

Water Pressure Drop At Every Fixture

Unlike a single-fixture pressure issue, a service line leak tends to reduce pressure house-wide since it affects the main supply before it ever reaches your internal plumbing.

Rising Water Bill With No Household Change

Especially when combined with a positive meter test, this is one of the strongest indicators of a service line leak specifically, rather than an internal leak.

Our Approach

How We Locate It Before Digging

We start with the same water meter test used for any hidden leak, then use electronic acoustic equipment to trace the sound of escaping water along the service line's path. This lets us target the excavation to the actual leak location rather than digging up the full run between the meter and the house on a guess.

FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if my leak is in the service line and not inside my house?

A meter test that shows movement, combined with normal water pressure and no signs of leaking anywhere inside the house, points toward the service line specifically. We confirm with acoustic tracing rather than assuming.

Who's responsible for repairing a leak in the service line, me or the water company?

In York, the property owner is generally responsible for the line from the meter to the house, while The York Water Company maintains the main up to the meter. We can help you confirm exactly where the responsibility boundary falls for your specific property.

Can a service line leak be repaired without replacing the whole line?

Often yes, if the leak is isolated to one section and the rest of the line is in reasonable condition. A line showing multiple failure points or significant age-related corrosion throughout may be better served by a full replacement instead.

How long can a service line leak run before it's a serious problem?

It varies, but a leak left unaddressed can waste significant water, erode soil around the line, and in freeze-thaw conditions, worsen faster than it would in a milder climate. Addressing it once confirmed is worth doing promptly rather than monitoring it.

Will fixing a service line leak lower my water bill back to normal?

Yes, once the leak is repaired, your usage should return to its normal baseline on your next billing cycle.

Can I run a test myself before calling?

Yes, the same water meter test described in our high water bill guide works here. If it confirms movement and you've ruled out an internal cause, that's the point to call for tracing and repair.
