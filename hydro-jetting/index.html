A drain that keeps clogging in the same spot even after a standard cable clearing usually means root intrusion in an older clay line or grease buildup coating the pipe walls, not a fresh clog. Hydro jetting is the answer in York, PA for exactly that situation: high-pressure water scours the full interior diameter of the pipe rather than just punching a hole through the clog, and we schedule most jetting calls within the week.

Local Context

Why Jetting Matters For York's Older Lines

Pre-1960 clay and cast-iron drain lines, common throughout downtown York, develop joint gaps over decades that let tree roots from mature street trees grow into the pipe. A cable can cut through roots temporarily, but jetting clears the growth and residue from the full pipe wall. This is common across North York, PA and the rest of York County too.

Signs To Watch For

Symptoms Of A Hydro Jetting Problem

Clog Returns Within Weeks

A drain that clears then clogs again quickly usually has root intrusion or heavy buildup a cable only partially addressed.

Multiple Slow Drains At Once

Several fixtures draining slowly points to a main line issue that benefits from a full jetting rather than spot cabling.

Camera Inspection Shows Buildup

If a camera inspection shows grease or scale coating the pipe walls, jetting clears it rather than just cutting a path through.

Sewage Smell From Drains

A persistent sewage odor from floor drains can indicate a partially blocked line that isn't draining fully.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is hydro jetting safe for old clay pipe in York?

Yes, when pressure is calibrated to the pipe's condition. We camera-inspect first to confirm the line can handle jetting before proceeding.

How is hydro jetting different from a regular drain cleaning?

Standard cabling cuts through a clog. Jetting scours the entire pipe wall, which is why it holds up longer against recurring root intrusion.

How often should older York lines be jetted?

For homes with a known root intrusion history, an annual or biannual jetting can prevent backups before they happen.

Will jetting damage my pipes?

Properly calibrated jetting is safe for most pipe materials in reasonable condition. We confirm the line's condition with a camera first.

Can jetting fix a collapsed pipe?

No. If a camera inspection shows a collapsed or severely offset section, that needs repair or replacement, not jetting, and we'll tell you plainly if that's what we see.
