Recurring pinhole leaks in different spots along the same line, rusty water, or pressure that's dropped steadily over years all point toward one underlying cause rather than a fixture-level problem: the supply line itself. A whole house repipe becomes the right call in York when a home's original supply lines, often galvanized steel in pre-1960 construction, have reached that point. We inspect the existing lines with a pressure test and visual assessment, usually scheduled within the week, then replace them with PEX or copper sized for the home's current fixtures rather than patching the same aging system repeatedly.

Local Context

Why Repiping Comes Up In Older York Homes

Galvanized steel supply lines, common in construction before the 1960s, corrode from the inside out over decades. The corrosion narrows the pipe's interior diameter, which shows up as gradually dropping water pressure long before an actual leak develops, and it eventually does develop leaks as the pipe walls thin. This is common across Valley Green, PA and the rest of York County too.

Signs To Watch For

Symptoms Of A Whole House Repipe Problem

Recurring Pinhole Leaks

Leaks appearing in different spots along the same supply line usually mean the whole pipe is corroding, not just one section.

Steadily Dropping Water Pressure

Pressure that has gotten worse over years rather than suddenly is a classic sign of internal pipe corrosion narrowing the line.

Rusty Or Discolored Water

Water that runs rusty, especially right after the house has been unused for a few hours, points to corroding galvanized pipe.

Visible Corrosion On Exposed Pipe

Flaking, rust-colored buildup on exposed supply lines in a basement is a visible version of what's likely happening throughout the system.

Our Approach

How A Repipe Project Works

We pressure-test the existing lines and map them room by room, plan routing that minimizes wall and ceiling openings, and replace the system with PEX or copper. Most whole-house repipes for a York rowhome or single-family home are completed in two to four days.

FAQs

Common Questions

How much does it cost to repipe an older York home?

Most whole-house repipes fall between $4,500 and $12,000 depending on the home's size, number of fixtures, and how accessible the existing lines are.

How long does a whole house repipe take?

Most York rowhomes and single-family homes are completed in two to four days, with water restored to at least part of the house each night.

Do I have to move out during a repipe?

Most homeowners stay in the home. We coordinate work room by room and keep water access restored overnight wherever possible.

Will a repipe damage my walls?

We plan routing to minimize openings and patch what we do open. Some drywall or plaster repair is normal, and we're upfront about where that will happen before starting.

Is PEX or copper better for a York home?

Both are solid choices. PEX is faster to install and handles freeze-related expansion slightly better, while copper has a longer track record. We'll walk through the tradeoffs for your specific home.

Can you repipe just part of the house instead of everything?

Yes, if only one section is failing we can scope a partial repipe rather than the full system.
