Why Your Boiler Is Banging (And The Fix For Each Cause)

A boiler that bangs or knocks when the heat kicks on is one of the most common calls we get from York's older housing stock. There are three distinct causes, each with a different sound and a different fix, and telling them apart is the difference between a five-minute DIY fix and a repair call. Here's how to identify which one you actually have.

Cause 1: Trapped Air (Sounds Like Gentle Knocking Or Gurgling)

This is the most common cause and the one you can usually fix yourself. Air gets trapped in radiators and pipes over time, especially after any work on the system or after the heat has been off for a while during warmer months. When water tries to move through a section with trapped air, it creates a knocking or gurgling sound, most noticeable right when the heat starts up.

How To Fix Trapped Air Yourself

  1. Turn off the heating system and let the radiators cool completely, since bleeding a hot radiator can cause a burn from escaping steam
  2. Locate the bleed valve, usually a small square-headed valve at the top corner of the radiator
  3. Hold a cloth or small container under the valve, then use a radiator key or flathead screwdriver to turn it slowly counterclockwise
  4. You'll hear a hissing sound as air escapes. Close the valve the moment steady water starts flowing instead of air, usually within a few seconds
  5. Repeat on every radiator in the house, working from the lowest floor to the highest
  6. Check your boiler's pressure gauge afterward. Bleeding radiators drops system pressure slightly, and most boilers need it between 1 and 1.5 bar when cold. If it's low, use the filling loop to top it back up per your unit's manual

Cause 2: Water Hammer (Sounds Like A Sudden Bang Or Gunshot)

A sharper, more sudden bang, sometimes described as a gunshot sound, is different from the gentler knock of trapped air. This is water hammer: a pressure spike when a valve closes quickly, sending a shockwave through the pipe. Bleeding radiators won't fix this, since it's a mechanical issue with a valve or the piping layout, not air in the system. If this is what you're hearing, it's worth a proper diagnosis rather than repeated bleeding attempts that won't resolve it.

Cause 3: Kettling (Sounds Like Rumbling Or A Kettle Coming To A Boil)

A rumbling, kettle-like sound is different again, and it means sediment or limescale has built up on the heat exchanger, similar to scale in an actual kettle. This traps water against the hot surface, causing it to boil locally and produce the rumbling sound. Kettling is worth taking seriously: it means the boiler is working harder than it should to heat the same amount of water, which shows up as higher fuel bills and accelerated wear on the heat exchanger.

Which One Do You Have? A Quick Decision Guide

  • Gentle knock or gurgle that gets quieter after the radiators have been running a few minutes: trapped air, bleed the radiators yourself
  • Sudden sharp bang, especially when a tap or valve elsewhere in the house closes: water hammer, needs a technician to look at valve timing or add a water hammer arrestor
  • Ongoing rumbling or kettle-like sound that doesn't go away after bleeding: kettling from sediment buildup, needs a system flush
  • Bled the radiators and the knocking is still there within a few days: air is likely re-entering through a leak or a faulty automatic air vent, worth a proper look

When To Stop Troubleshooting And Call Someone

Bleeding radiators yourself is safe and straightforward. Diagnosing water hammer or kettling on your own generally isn't, since both point to an issue inside the system that needs proper equipment to confirm and fix. If you've bled every radiator and you're still hearing banging, or the sound is a sharp bang rather than a gentle knock, that's the point to call rather than keep guessing through a York winter.
