Local Context

Why York Drains Clog More Than Average

Clay and cast-iron pipe, common under pre-1960 York homes, develops rough interior surfaces and joint gaps over decades that catch debris and let tree roots in. Row houses in the historic core sharing a single lateral line add a complication unique to this market: a clog can originate at a neighboring property and still back up into your basement. Signs To Watch For

Symptoms Of A Drain Cleaning Problem

Slow Draining Sinks Or Tubs

Multiple fixtures draining slowly at once usually points to a main line issue rather than one clogged trap.

Gurgling Sounds

A gurgling toilet or drain when another fixture is used is a sign of a partial blockage further down the line.

Backup Into A Floor Drain

Water backing up into a basement floor drain is one of the clearest signs of a main line clog, not a fixture-level one.

Recurring Clogs In The Same Spot

A drain that clears temporarily and clogs again within weeks usually has a root intrusion or structural issue, not just debris.

FAQs

Common Questions

Why do my drains clog so often in an older York home?

Pre-1960 clay and cast-iron lines develop rough interior surfaces and joint gaps over time, and mature street trees common in older neighborhoods send roots into those gaps.

Can you clear a drain shared with my neighbor's row house?

Yes. We locate where the shared lateral connects and can camera-inspect it to find whether the clog is on your side or theirs.

Is hydro jetting safe for old pipe?

Yes, when done at the right pressure for the pipe material. We assess the line's condition before jetting an older clay or cast-iron run.

What's the difference between cabling, snaking, and jetting?

Cabling and snaking describe the same basic method: a flexible cable cuts through a clog. Jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the full pipe wall instead. We use a cable for most standard kitchen and bathroom sink clogs, and move to jetting when a line keeps recurring.

My kitchen sink backs up every few months, is that normal?

Recurring kitchen sink backups usually mean grease or food debris building up in the same section of pipe repeatedly, which a one-time cabling doesn't fully address. A jetting service clears the buildup along the full pipe wall rather than just cutting a path through it.
