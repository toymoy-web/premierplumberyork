A water bill that's climbed with no change in your household's usage is one of the clearest signs something is wrong, even when there's nothing visible to point to. Before assuming the worst, we start with your water meter itself: a simple test with everything in the house turned off tells us within 30 minutes whether water is actually moving somewhere in your system. From there we narrow down the source using the same electronic leak tracing equipment we'd use for any hidden leak, rather than guessing or recommending unnecessary work.

Local Context

What Usually Turns Out To Be The Cause

In York's older housing stock, a rising bill without a visible leak most often traces back to a running toilet flapper, a slow leak in a supply line inside a wall, or, less commonly, a leak in the underground line between the meter and the house. Pre-1960 homes with original galvanized supply lines are more likely to be the source than newer construction, simply because the pipe itself is older and more prone to a slow pinhole leak. This is common across Valley Green, PA and the rest of York County too.

Signs To Watch For

Symptoms Of A High Water Bill Investigation Problem

Water Bill Increase With No Usage Change

A bill that's climbed for two consecutive cycles without any change in household water use is the strongest single signal worth investigating.

Water Meter Still Moving With Everything Off

If your meter's low-flow indicator is spinning with every fixture in the house off, water is moving somewhere in your system right now.

A Specific Area That's Always Damp

A consistently damp patch of lawn, a musty spot in a closet, or a warm patch on the floor can point toward where the leak actually is.

An Unusually Quiet House Sound

Water audible when the house should be silent, most noticeable late at night, is worth paying attention to rather than dismissing.

Our Approach

How We Actually Find It

We start with the water meter test: record the reading, wait 30 minutes with no water use anywhere in the house, then check again. Any movement confirms a leak exists. From there we rule out the easy, cheap causes first (a running toilet, a dripping fixture) before using electronic and acoustic leak tracing equipment to narrow down anything hidden inside a wall, under a floor, or in the underground service line.

FAQs

Common Questions

Why is my water bill so high with no leak I can see?

Most hidden leaks never show up as visible water. A running toilet flapper alone can waste hundreds of gallons a month silently, and a slow supply-line leak inside a wall can run for weeks before it's visible. The meter test tells you definitively whether one exists.

How do I read my own water meter to check for a leak?

Locate the meter, usually near the street in a small ground-level box or in the basement on older York homes. Make sure nothing in the house is using water, note the reading including the small low-flow indicator dial, wait 30 minutes, then check again. Any change means water is moving somewhere.

Is a high water bill covered by insurance?

No, a high bill on its own isn't an insurable event. If the underlying cause turns out to be a leak that damaged your home, that damage may be covered depending on the source, which is a separate conversation from the bill itself.

How much water does a running toilet actually waste?

A running toilet can waste over 200 gallons a day in some cases, which shows up fast on a water bill even though nothing looks obviously wrong.

Can The York Water Company confirm a leak from their side?

They can sometimes flag unusual usage patterns, but they can't locate a leak on your side of the meter. That's the diagnostic step we handle.

What if the meter test shows no movement but my bill is still high?

That usually means the usage is real, not a leak, often from an appliance, irrigation, or a change in household habits worth reviewing before assuming a hidden problem.
